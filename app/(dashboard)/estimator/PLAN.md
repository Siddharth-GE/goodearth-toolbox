# Estimator — the rules

**Built 2026-08-19.** Migrations `0074`–`0088` and `0096` (with `0078`–`0081` shared with Indents and Inventory; `0086` folded the material list into the items master). Grant: `/estimator`.

What a villa costs to build. Works come from the Masters vocabulary (`0073`); this tool adds what they are measured in, what the labour costs, and what they consume — materials directly, or through a named mix several works share. An estimate is a villa and a list of works with quantities; from that the tool computes the material takeoff and the money.

## The founder's decisions

1. **Materials ARE the items master — there is no material list here at all** (founder, 2026-08-20, in two steps the same day: first `0085` made every new material start from a picked item; then "we don't even need a materials tab — all materials are exactly the same as in the items master. Mixes are a combination of items; a work just has those items", `0086`). The **rate is `items.indicative_price`**, edited in Masters and as visible as item prices always were; the estimate's computed totals stay `/estimator`-gated. **One unit language**: recipe quantities are in the item's own `default_uom` (sand in cft, not cum), so nothing ever converts and issued-vs-estimated compares exactly. `estimator_mix_components` and `estimator_work_components` carry `item_id`; the snapshot freezes `item_id`, the item's name/unit and the price of the day. `estimator_materials` is retired in place — never dropped, and since 2026-09-26 **never read by the Estimator either**: a recipe or mix row from before `0086` names no item, counts for nothing and shows as "An older material row — no longer counted" with its Remove button; a frozen takeoff row from before it shows by its frozen name and is not compared. `estimate_takeoff_facts` still bridges both generations for the site tools behind one unchanged column list (`coalesce(t.item_id, m.item_id)`; a conversion factor only ever attaches to legacy rows). Both databases were surveyed before the change: production had zero estimator rows; staging's two unconvertible practice components stayed legacy on purpose — nothing was guessed.
2. **A work can vary per villa** (founder, 2026-08-20: "every house is different — there may be some variations"; `0087`). The Works tab stays the STANDARD; inside a draft estimate, Variation → Customise copies the standard recipe onto that line, and from then on the line's own list — whole, not a delta — is what it means: it stops following the standard, later standard edits leave it alone, and Reset (or removing the last material) returns it to standard. The **labour rate and the material prices vary per villa too** (`0088`, founder the same day: "sometimes labour variations can happen in a plot as well [as] prices"), and each sits where its variation belongs: labour on the estimate LINE (one line per work, so the line is the work-in-this-villa), a material price on the ESTIMATE (cement costing more at a far plot costs more for every work that uses it — one row per material makes two works contradicting each other impossible). Both are overrides, not copies: blank means the master shows through, and 0 is a real rate, never "unpriced". Only the work's UNIT still cannot vary — it defines what every quantity on the line means. Variations travel with Revise and with template copies (`copyLineVariations`), die with their line, and need no downstream plumbing: the 0077 snapshot freezes the computed result, so pulls, comparisons and warnings read exactly what they always read.
3. **Mixes are named and reusable**, and a work's recipe may hold both mixes and direct materials. Change M20 once and every concrete work follows.
4. **Money is in from day one** — material rates, labour rates, totals.
5. **One working estimate per villa; Make official takes a frozen copy** (founder, 2026-09-26: "one estimate per villa", after "each villa is different"; `0098`). This replaced "an estimate starts as a copy of a template" — the 43 villas are not near-identical. A villa has exactly ONE working estimate (`is_working`, a partial unique index), always a draft, always editable. **Make official** (`make_estimate_official`) copies it into a new header with everything under it, writes the snapshot the app computed, refuses if the snapshot no longer matches what it copied, and calls `submit_estimate` to mint EST/…/NNN and supersede the previous official — one transaction; the working estimate stays open. Revise, Copy to villa and templates are gone from the screens (`is_template` stays in the schema, and staging and production have none). A villa starts blank or **from another estimate** (`start_villa_estimate`): another villa's list of works, each to measure, or — ticked — everything, which is how a villa carries on from its own official. Drafts from before `0098` that are not a villa's working estimate show as "older draft", deletable, never made official.
6. **Units are picked, not typed** (first-use feedback, 2026-08-19: "get a units master, we cant have people type all this"). Since `0082` the list is the SHARED Masters one (`uoms`, managed at Masters → Units) — the founder's 2026-08-20 "keep it in the masters" ended the tool's private list (`estimator_uoms` stays as a table, nothing reads it). The uom columns now carry a NOT VALID FK to the master: renames cascade, history is excused, and the 0077 snapshot columns stay free text because they are frozen copies.
7. **The estimate reads as a BOQ** (same feedback round: "the ui of the final estimate isnt simple… think about how an estimator will use it"). One grand total on top, works grouped under their categories with subtotals, one Rate and one Amount column per line, add-a-work behind a button. `groupLineCosts` in `calc.ts` produces that grouping, and anything that later prints an estimate must render its output rather than grouping again.
8. **A line's quantity can come from a measurement sheet** (founder, 2026-09-25: "a qs layer where you can enter the number of lengths and volumes for each work"; `0096`). Every work on a draft has a Measure button; the sheet is one row per wall, slab or footing with Nos, Length, Breadth and Depth, and **a blank box is simply not used** — "12 nos" is 12, "1 × 4 × 3 × 0.15" is 1.8. **Additions only**, by the founder's choice: no deduction rows, openings are measured net by hand. The quantity **stays on `estimator_estimate_lines.qty`** — after every row change the action re-sums the sheet through `calc.ts` (`measurementQuantity`/`sheetTotal`, rounded to six decimals) and writes it there, so submit, the 0077 snapshot, indent pulls and every comparison read exactly what they always read. A measured line's quantity box goes read-only and `updateEstimateLineQty` refuses it; removing the last row leaves the total behind as a typed quantity. Rows are draft-only in the database (the 0087 trigger shape), so after submit they stand as the record of where the frozen quantity came from — no snapshot copy needed. Sheets travel with template copies and Revise (`copyLineMeasurements`) and die with their line.
9. **No scheduling.** The site team's workbook also tracks dates and delays per plot; the founder cut that from this build ("this whole build is only estimation"). Relay is where progress lives today, and it deliberately stores no per-activity dates — if scheduling is ever built, that gap is where it goes, and `relay/PLAN.md` already reserves "unit stages" for the house screen.
10. **Every villa is different, and a rate explains itself** (founder, 2026-09-26: "each villa is different", "finishes are different, foundations can be different"; the rework in root `plan.md`). There is no house type that villas follow: what is shared is **how a work is priced** — the **rate book** (the Works tab, renamed: unit, labour rate, materials per unit at Masters' prices) — never how much of it a villa has. On a draft, **every rate is a button** that opens its build-up for this villa: labour, what one unit uses, what each material costs, each figure marked _Rate book_ or _This villa_. Different foundations are different works (the list already has rubble / isolated / pile); a different finish is **Swap** on the villa's row — the rate book's material stands in as an allowance until the villa's choice is made. Editing a villa's materials is **copy-on-write** (`changeLineRecipe`): the first change copies the rate book's list onto the line, so the separate Customise step is gone; ↺ Back to the rate book deletes it. Someone holding `/masters` can set a missing Masters price from the same panel (`setItemPrice`, the one narrow Masters write, gated `/masters` like every other). The rate book lists the works **used on an estimate but not priced** — the setup that matters — and offers **Copy this rate to…** with the same work on other floors ticked (`floorTwins`, `works-rules.ts`). Selections and Budgets stay interiors and out of this.

## The rules everything rests on

- **Every table here is `/estimator`-gated, SELECT included** (`0074`, the `0058` funding_facilities shape). Masters reads are ungated by design, so a table carrying a rate cannot live there — that is why a work's unit and labour rate are in `estimator_work_info` and not on `work_items`. **Works stay money-free in Masters.** Beyond shared surfaces — the works vocabulary, `projects`, `units`, `profiles` (the submitter's name, `0077`), and the `items` master, which since `0086` IS the material list — it reads one other tool: Inventory's open, money-free `stock_issues(_lines)` and `goods_receipts(_lines)`, for issued-vs-estimated. Inventory never reads the Estimator's tables.
- **A missing rate is `null`, and `null` is never zero.** `formatMoney(null)` prints "—", the calculator returns `null` for any cost it cannot know, and the screens say "not priced". A confident ₹0 in a total is the failure this codebase keeps naming; the nullable return type is the only version of that rule the compiler can enforce.
- **All arithmetic is in `lib/estimator/calc.ts`** — pure, import-free, 36 tests. The per-work cost on the Works tab and the per-line cost on an estimate come from the same `computeLine`, so they cannot disagree.
- **Costs are live WHILE DRAFT, frozen at submit** (`0077`). A draft stores quantities only and recomputes from today's rates — the accepted cost that a draft's total moves when a rate does is unchanged. Submit writes the snapshot: per-line costs (`estimator_estimate_line_costs`) and the per-(work, material) takeoff (`estimator_estimate_takeoff`), computed by `calc.ts` in the app and validated-not-recomputed by `submit_estimate()` — SQL never re-implements the arithmetic. A submitted estimate renders from its snapshot through the same `groupLineCosts` path (`frozenLineCosts`/`aggregateFrozenTakeoff` in calc.ts), and null stays null in the freeze: unpriced on the day of submit reads as unpriced forever, never ₹0.
- **A template is an estimate with no villa**, and the database says so: `check (is_template = (unit_id is null))`. No screen makes one since `0098`; the rule stays true for the schema.
- **One working estimate and one OFFICIAL estimate per villa** (`0098`'s `estimator_estimates_working_key`, `0077`'s `estimator_estimates_official_key`). Make official supersedes the villa's previous official inside the same transaction; a race between two loses at the index, loudly. Only drafts can be edited or deleted (`delete_draft_estimate`, one transaction) — lines, snapshot and header are all guarded in the database. **A quantity can be null** (`0097`): "to measure", listed before it is measured; Make official refuses while any is, so no frozen or downstream quantity is ever null.
- **Deletion is refused, not cascaded.** A material used in a mix or recipe cannot be deleted (RESTRICT) — deactivate it. The delete button only appears where nothing uses the row.
- **Unplanned material is flagged forever, and approval never clears the flag** (`0083`, founder 2026-08-20: a PO "not part of an estimate… needs to be clearly flagged like a reconciliation… and it sits in the estimate forever with that flag"). The DETECTION stays derived — `compare.ts`'s unmatched bucket, per (work, item), computed live from issues (`0080`) and direct-to-site deliveries (`0081`) — so nothing depends on anyone remembering to write a row. Only the estimator's acknowledgement is stored (`estimator_reconciliation_approvals`): approving records who looked and why it was needed; the amber "Outside the estimate" badge stays on the official estimate permanently either way. Anchored to the estimate, not the villa — a revision that plans the material clears the entry the honest way (the takeoff now accounts for it); one that doesn't asks its estimator again. There is deliberately no delete policy on approvals and no un-approve button.
- **An approval carries to the villa's later officials** (founder, 2026-09-26, once Make official became routine): an outside-the-estimate arrival approved on any of a villa's official estimates stays approved on the next, matched on (work, item). The flag still never clears. This relaxes the `0083` rule that a new official asks again — read-side only (`getReconciliationApprovals`, `getApprovalsFor`), no schema change.
- **No embeds.** The estimates list needs villa names and `units` has two FK paths to `plots` since `0029`, so names are merged through a `Map`. A bare embed compiles, builds, and answers HTTP 300 at runtime (BUGCATCHER #2).

## Things that will bite

- **Changing a work's unit after estimate lines exist** silently changes what every one of those quantities means — 40 "cum" becoming 40 "sqm" is the same number describing a different building. The setup form warns with the line count, but it does not refuse; nothing in the database can tell the difference.
- **An official must not point back at the working estimate it was made from.** `source_estimate_id` is ON DELETE SET NULL, and the `0077` guard refuses any update to a submitted header — so deleting a working estimate an official pointed at would fail on the cascade's UPDATE. `make_estimate_official` leaves the official's source null on purpose. Any future "made from" link needs its own column, not that one.
- **`uom` values come from the shared Masters list** (`0082` — a NOT VALID FK; a saved value no longer in the master is still offered when editing that row, so old rows stay saveable, and updates that don't touch the uom never re-check it). Nothing stops two units meaning the same thing (cft and cuft) if someone adds both in Masters. **The arithmetic never converts between units** — a component's quantity is in its own unit, per one unit of its parent, and the screens print "bags per cum" so the meaning is visible.
- **The measurement sheet never converts units** (`0096`). The boxes are multiplied as typed, so a length in feet on a cum work, or a depth in mm, produces a confident wrong quantity. The work's unit shown beside the total is the only guard. Likewise, the sheet's total reaches the line in a second write after the row write; if that second write fails the action says so, and the next change to any row re-syncs it.
- **A mix with no materials contributes nothing** rather than erroring. The mixes list and the work recipe both flag "nothing in it yet" — that flag is the only thing standing between an empty mix and a quietly cheap estimate.

## Later, if asked

Estimate against actual on the money side (POs and bills) — the quantity side ships with the backbone: the official estimate already compares issued material per work. The works, POs and bills exist to compare with, and that comparison is the reason to build this on the shared works list rather than a private one.

The founder has sketched the reporting surface to come (2026-08-19), and the pieces already line up under it — none of it needs new arithmetic:

- **An estimate PDF** renders `groupLineCosts` + `computeTakeoff` through `lib/pdf/` — the same structures the screen draws, so paper and screen can never disagree.
- **A per-work report (rate analysis / BOQ line detail)** is the work's setup page: unit, labour rate, recipe expanded, cost per unit. A print of it reuses `computeLine` with qty 1.
- **Material and labour usage filters** — "where does all the cement go", "labour across Saarang" — sum `LineCost.labourCost` / `expandRecipe` output across estimates, filtered by category, work or villa. Every number needed is already computed per line; the filters are a screen, not a model change.
